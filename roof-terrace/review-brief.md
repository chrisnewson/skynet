# Roof Terrace Planting — brief for an adversarial design review

**Flat 6, 22 Sussex Square, Brighton BN2 5AA** · 30 August 2026

> **What we want from you: to be told what is wrong with this.**
>
> The scheme below has been developed in detail but has **never been seen by a second designer, and its
> author has never visited the site.** Before it goes to a nursery for pricing we would like it attacked
> by someone who has no investment in it.
>
> Please do not be diplomatic. A review that says "broadly sound with minor observations" is worth
> nothing to us. §5 lists the specific claims we would most like you to try to break.

---

## 1. The site in one paragraph

A new roof terrace of about 120 m² on the third floor of a Grade I listed Regency building on the Kemp
Town seafront, within the Kemp Town Enclosures. Roughly **26.4 m²** is planted, in timber planters built
on top of new granite paving, plus **25 pots**. Full sun almost throughout; salt wind from all four
directions with no shelter; lightweight free-draining roof-garden mix 400–1,000mm deep; automatic drip
irrigation. The scheme forms part of a Listed Building Application with a biodiversity requirement. **The
client will be planting it himself, single-handed, with no vehicle or lift access to the roof.**

## 3. What is fixed, and not worth your time

These are settled by other parties or by the client, and challenging them will not change anything:

- **Planter positions, footprints and heights.** Set by the architect and already in the builder's tender.
- **The roof build-up, tiling and pedestal system**, and the fact that planters sit on top of finished
  tiles rather than being built into the build-up.
- **Two-phase delivery.** The sauna, hot tub, parasols and fall-protection glass are Phase 2, subject to
  Listed Building Consent. **The planting goes in during Phase 1, so for its first year-plus there is no
  glass and no built shelter.**
- **The cool colour palette** — silver foliage, blue/purple/white flowers — is a client decision.
- **Four olive trees**, which carry a heritage-continuity argument in the planning application.
- **Drip irrigation is being installed.**

## 5. The claims we would most like you to try to break

These are the load-bearing arguments in the plan. If any of them is wrong, the scheme is wrong.

### On wind
1. **"A ~50% porous barrier shelters 10–15× its height downwind; a solid one shelters less and throws an
   eddy."** The entire back-row species logic rests on this. Is it being applied correctly at this scale —
   a 1.5m shrub row in a 600mm planter — or is it agronomy-textbook reasoning imported into a situation
   where it doesn't hold?
2. **Can 26 m² of planting in shallow containers meaningfully shelter a 120 m² roof terrace at all**, or
   is that wishful? Should we be honest that only the Phase-2 glass will do it?
3. The narrow terrace has **open steel railings, not a parapet** — wind passes through below 1,100mm. Is
   the response (planting as the entire windbreak from ground level) adequate?

### On the no-clipping rule
4. The palette excludes **any plant that needs clipping to hold its shape**, because no planter's back
   face can be reached and clippings would fall on neighbours. *Griselinia*, *Elaeagnus* and taller
   *Escallonia* were removed for this reason. **Is that over-cautious, and has it cost the scheme its best
   windbreak plants for a manageable maintenance problem?**
5. A **mature-spread rule** — no shrub wider than planter depth + ~300mm each side — follows from the same
   logic. Is it too restrictive?

### On the sightline geometry
6. **Two crown-lifted *Arbutus unedo*, canopies clear from ~1.6m, are supposed to block a neighbour's
   downward sightline into the hot tub while leaving the horizontal view out untouched.** This has been
   reasoned, **not drawn or surveyed**. Does the geometry actually work? Is *Arbutus* the right tree?
7. **M2(E)'s north-half understorey is capped at 300mm and M4 at 1m** to protect views of the Downs.
   **Will either read as designed planting, or as a token?**

### On horticulture
8. **Gravel mulch, slow-release feed, and no compost on the Mediterranean beds** — justified as keeping
   crowns dry and keeping growth lean and wind-firm. Is the crown-rot argument sound in containers, or is
   this a ground-level gravel-garden principle misapplied to pots?
9. **Snowdrops confined to the part-shaded south end** on the grounds that summer bake, not drought, is
   what kills them and irrigation cannot fix it. Right?
10. **Species tulips specified because they perennialise** in gritty dry containers. Do they, here, in
    year five?
11. **211 plants over 26.4 m²**, on assumptions of a back row at 0.8–1.0m centres and a front ribbon at
    ~2.5 plants per linear metre. **Over-planted, under-planted, or right?**
12. The scheme is **heavily evergreen**. Does it lack seasonal drama? Will it look static and dull?
13. **Two irrigation zones** for 26 m² of planter plus 25 pots including mint. Enough?

### On deliverability
14. Everything comes up **by rope haul or crane**, and **one person is planting it**. Is the specification
    realistic on that basis — particularly seven specimens at 25–70kg?
15. **Green waste has to be carried down through the flat.** The scheme claims to produce ~300–400 litres
    of soft prunings a year and proposes a 100-litre hot composter. Realistic?
16. **Terracotta pots on a roof** — 25 of them, filled, the largest 50–70kg each. Right material, or a
    load and frost problem waiting to happen?

## 6. Where the author is least confident — start here

Stated plainly, because it is where you are most likely to find something:

| Uncertainty | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **The site has never been visited.** Everything is from drawings, photographs and the client's descriptions | Aspect, shade, wind funnelling and sightlines are all inferred |
| **Aspect and shade are not measured.** "Full sun except the south end" is the client's read | If there is more shade than assumed, a large part of the palette is wrong |
| **Parapet and railing heights are the client's figures, not surveyed** | Every height cap in the plan derives from them |
| **⚠️ An unresolved conflict in the source documents.** Part C states M2 is "700mm above FA3 FFL"; the client confirms M2 sits on the *raised* level. Both cannot be true | If the box is 400mm rather than 700mm, the M2(E) north planting is wrong by half its height |
| **Loading has not been re-checked** against the structural engineer's figures. The calcs allow 350 kg/m² for planters; the make-up is estimated at 250–350, and 25mm of gravel adds ~42 kg/m² | Could exceed the allowance. A mitigation is proposed (drop the soil 25mm) but nobody has verified the sum |
| **Wind exposure is inferred from geography, not measured** | No anemometry, no wind study |
| **The plant list has not been checked for availability** at the sizes specified | Lead times on multi-stem crown-lifted *Arbutus* in particular |

---

## 7. What would make your review most useful

1. **Lead with the things that are actually wrong**, ranked by consequence — not by how easy they are to
   say.
2. **Say which of the §5 claims survive and which don't**, and why.
3. **Where you disagree, name the alternative.** "This won't work" is half a review.
4. **Tell us what a good designer would have done differently at the level of the whole scheme**, not just
   plant swaps — if the concept is wrong, say so.
5. **Flag anything that will fail in years 3–10** rather than year 1. Establishment is well covered; long-term
   is not.
6. If you think **the client should not be planting this himself**, say that too.
